In-space chemical propulsion · In development

Propulsion systems shaped around the mission.

Aeterna is developing bipropellant chemical propulsion systems for spacecraft-specific configurations. Thruster arrangements, propellant storage and delivery, and propulsion controls are considered together around maneuvering and integration requirements.

Intended offering

The thruster is a building block.

The intended customer offering is a propulsion subsystem configured for the spacecraft. Initial development focuses on a thruster and its feed-system interfaces; the broader product direction includes configurations with multiple thrusters and different operating roles.

Thruster number and arrangement, storage and feed layout, propulsion controls, and spacecraft interfaces are configuration choices to be developed against mission requirements. Initial thruster development does not define a single fixed package for every customer.

Supported configurations and operating limits will be established through development and verification. A change in arrangement or operating role must be assessed for its effect on the system.

Development priorities

From individual firings to repeated spacecraft operations.

Our priorities are development objectives, not demonstrated specifications:

  • Repeated operation: establish supported firing sequences, restart conditions, and cumulative operating life.
  • Predictable pulses: characterize the impulse delivered by a commanded firing, its repeatability, and its dependence on operating conditions.
  • Sustained firing: develop the feed and thermal design for longer burns alongside short-pulse operation.
  • Spacecraft integration: define propulsion command, power, mechanical, and thermal interfaces alongside the intended thruster configurations.

A configuration-level engineering question

Which thrusters need to fire together?

A multi-thruster configuration needs a defined set of firing combinations and sequences. Our configuration assessment will consider the propellant delivery and electrical demand of those combinations, as well as the interfaces and operating limits of each thruster.

Valve operation can produce feed-line pressure transients, a documented consideration in propulsion-system analysis. NASA’s technical brief on propellant feed-system transients provides background on that interaction.

For Aeterna, the development question is what firing combinations the selected feed and control configuration can support. Individual-thruster characterization and configuration-level verification are therefore separate parts of the planned work.

Development status

Initial thruster work. A broader system objective.

The program is in design and analysis, before prototype fabrication and testing. Manufacturing, assembly, inspection, and verification planning are part of the current engineering scope; qualification and flight readiness have not been established.

  1. 01

    Design and analysis

    Current

    Investigate propellant delivery, thruster behavior, and thermal management alongside fabrication and assembly constraints. Define what the first hardware tests need to measure.

  2. 02

    Thruster prototype testing

    Planned

    Characterize an initial thruster under controlled ground-supply conditions, including the firing behavior and operating limits needed to inform system development.

  3. 03

    Integrated configuration testing

    Future

    Assess the selected source/feed system, propulsion controls, and thruster arrangement together against their intended operating sequences and spacecraft interfaces.

  4. 04

    Configuration-specific verification

    Future

    Extend testing to relevant environments, endurance, and qualification requirements for the intended configuration and use.
